# Hotpress Pricing Model

> Philosophy: Cover costs, don't extract. Free tier is genuinely useful. Paid = professional polish.

---

## Final Pricing Structure

### Free Forever

Everything you need to write and publish:

- Unlimited writing & real-time collaboration
- Unlimited projects
- Unlimited PDF/EPUB exports (iterate freely on same book)
- Basic templates
- "Made with Hotpress" footer on exports

**Cost to us:** ~$0.03/active room (Liveblocks) + ~$0.05/export (compute)

---

### Creator — $12.99 per book (one-time)

When you're ready to publish professionally:

- Clean exports (no watermark/footer)
- Print-ready CMYK with bleed marks
- All premium templates for that book
- Unlimited exports of that book forever

**Target user:** Casual authors, first-time publishers, hobbyists

---

### Pro — $39.99/year

For serious authors who publish regularly:

- Clean exports on all projects
- All premium templates
- CMYK print-ready on everything
- GitHub sync for manuscript versioning
- No per-book fees ever

**Target user:** Indie authors, serial publishers, writing professionals

## Research & Reasoning

### What Makes Pricing Feel Parasitic

From extensive research on creator economy platforms, these patterns make creators feel exploited:

| Parasitic | Creator-Friendly |
|-----------|------------------|
| Platform profits regardless of creator success | Platform only wins when you win |
| Hidden fees, escalating costs at scale | Transparent, predictable pricing |
| Lock-in (proprietary formats, no export) | You own everything, can leave anytime |
| Subscription ticks during creative breaks | Respects creative rhythms |
| Feature-gating basic tools behind paywalls | Equal access to core features |
| Penalizing iteration (pay per export) | Iterate freely, pay for final value |

Key insight from Ted Gioia's "Parasite Economy" critique: platforms that consume creator content without creating value themselves are the problem. Spotify's CEO is richer than any musician in history while artists struggle.

### Models Creators Actually Love

**1. Pay Once, Own Forever (Vellum, Scrivener, Atticus)**
- Vellum: $249 one-time, unlimited books forever
- Scrivener: $59 one-time
- Atticus: $147 one-time

Writers adore this. No clock ticking. No guilt during breaks. Pay once, own it.

**2. Revenue Share, Zero Upfront (Substack, Leanpub)**
- Substack: 10% of paid subscriptions only
- Leanpub: 20% (but 80% royalty is exceptional)

Key: they only make money when you make money. Zero risk to start.

**3. Flat Fee, Zero Revenue Share (Ghost)**
- Ghost: $29/mo, keeps 100% of your revenue
- Non-profit foundation, no VCs, no pressure to extract
- Open source: self-host for free

Best for creators earning $500+/month (crossover point where flat beats %).

## Competitor Analysis

### Typeshare
- **Pricing:** $20/mo Pro, $200/year Creator
- **Model:** SaaS subscription, part of Ship 30 for 30 ecosystem
- **What works:** Generous free tier, no take rate on creator earnings, founders "eat their own cooking"
- **Philosophy:** Free content as foundation, validation through friction

### Substack
- **Pricing:** Free to use, 10% of paid subscription revenue + Stripe fees (~13% total)
- **Model:** Revenue share
- **What works:** Zero upfront cost, writers own subscriber lists completely, no lock-in
- **Philosophy:** "Everyone's incentives are aligned: Substack, the writers, and the users"
- **Criticism:** At scale, 10% is expensive ($50k/year = $5k to Substack)

### Ghost
- **Pricing:** Self-hosted FREE, Ghost(Pro) from $15/mo, 0% revenue share
- **Model:** Flat subscription, non-profit
- **What works:** Zero revenue share, open source, non-profit structure means no VC pressure
- **Philosophy:** "Why would you run a for-profit corporation?" - John O'Nolan

### Leanpub
- **Pricing:** 80% royalty, no monthly fees, variable pricing model
- **Model:** Revenue share on sales
- **What works:** 80% royalty is exceptional, "publish early, publish often," readers can pay MORE than minimum
- **Philosophy:** Books take time to write; no subscription fees that make authors feel "on a clock"

### Gumroad
- **Pricing:** No monthly fees, 10% + $0.50 per transaction, 30% on Discover sales
- **Model:** Revenue share
- **What works:** No upfront costs, handles global tax obligations
- **What broke trust:** Hidden price increases, sudden policy changes, replacing support with AI
- **Lesson:** Communication and consistency matter as much as the model itself

### Vellum (Book Formatting)
- **Pricing:** $199.99 ebook, $249.99 ebook + print, one-time
- **Model:** Pay once, own forever
- **What works:** Free until you generate, unlimited books forever, "just works" reliability
- **Limitation:** Mac only

### Atticus (Book Formatting)
- **Pricing:** $147 one-time, cross-platform
- **Model:** Pay once, own forever
- **What works:** Cheaper than Vellum, works everywhere, all future updates included
- **Philosophy:** Built by Kindlepreneur for indie authors

### Reedsy
- **Pricing:** Free core features, $4.99-10.99/mo for premium, 20% marketplace commission
- **Model:** Freemium + marketplace
- **What works:** Core writing/formatting genuinely free, export to pro formats at no cost
- **Monetization:** Vetted marketplace of editors, designers, marketers

### Patreon Lessons
- **2017 disaster:** Proposed shifting fees to patrons, massive backlash
- **Key lesson:** "Creators should own the business decisions with their fans, not Patreon"
- **What they learned:** Don't inject yourself into creator-audience relationship

## Models We Considered & Rejected

### Usage-Based / Hybrid Credits
- Base subscription + pay for AI/exports
- Example: $9/mo + $0.002/AI word, $0.50/export
- **Rejected:** Feels nickel-and-dime, penalizes iteration

### Collaboration-Based (Coda Model)
- Charge for "editors," viewers free
- Example: $12/editor/month
- **Rejected:** Real-time collab isn't our moat—Google Docs is free. We lose to PiratePad.

### Per-Export Pricing
- $2.99 per PDF, $2.99 per EPUB
- **Rejected:** Penalizes iteration. Exporting 10 times to fix typos isn't 10x the value—it's ONE book.

### Export Packs
- 10 exports for $19.99
- **Rejected:** Same problem. Shouldn't charge more for same book publication. Unused credits feel wasteful.

### Monthly Subscription
- $19.99/month unlimited
- **Rejected:** Subscribe → export → cancel pattern likely. Doesn't respect creative rhythms.

### Revenue Share
- 5-8% of book sales
- **Rejected:** Complex tracking, requires integrations, delayed revenue, relies on user honesty

### Room-Based Pricing
- Pay per project, collaborators free
- **Rejected:** Still charges for collaboration which isn't our differentiator

### Network Discount Model
- Price drops as your writing network grows
- **Rejected:** Complex, unpredictable revenue, gameable

### Writers' Guild / Cooperative
- Groups pool subscription costs
- **Rejected:** Too complex to implement and explain

### Per-Book at $19.99
- One-time unlock per project
- **Rejected:** Too expensive for casual users writing their first book

## Cost Analysis

### Liveblocks (Real-time Collaboration)

| Tier | Base | Rooms Included | Overage |
|------|------|----------------|---------|
| Free | $0 | 500 monthly active rooms | — |
| Pro | $30/mo | 500 rooms | +$0.03/room |

**Key insight:** They charge per room, not per user. Each user creating 2-3 sessions means ~150-200 active users on free tier before costs kick in.

**Strategic note:** Real-time collab is table stakes, not a moat. Users will use Google Docs for free collab and only come to Hotpress for export if we charge for collab.

### PDF/EPUB Generation

Hotpress uses React-PDF (self-hosted), not external APIs:
- External APIs (DocRaptor, PDFShift): $0.03-0.12 per document
- Self-hosted React-PDF: ~$0.02-0.05 per export (server CPU time)

### Estimated Cost Per User

| Activity | Cost |
|----------|------|
| Active room (Liveblocks) | ~$0.03/room/month |
| PDF export | ~$0.02-0.05 |
| EPUB export | ~$0.01-0.03 |
| Database/hosting | ~$0.50-1.00/active user/month |

**Bottom line:** Marginal cost per export is ~$0.05-0.10. Very low.

### $19.99 Scenario Modeling (Reference)

We modeled four scenarios at $19.99:

**Per Book:** 97% margin, but feels expensive for casuals
**10 Export Pack:** 95-98% margin, but penalizes iteration
**Monthly:** 90% margin, but subscribe-cancel pattern
**Annual:** 40-92% margin depending on usage, good for growth but prolific users unprofitable

Settled on $12.99/book as more accessible entry point.

## Why This Model Works

### Not Parasitic
- Free tier publishes real books, not crippled demos
- No per-export fees that punish iteration
- One-time unlock per book, not recurring extraction
- Pro is genuinely good value for active publishers
- You own your content, can export and leave anytime

### Covers Costs
- Free users cost ~$0.50-2/year (minimal active usage)
- $12.99/book covers infrastructure with healthy margin (~90%+)
- $39.99/year from Pro users sustains development

### Respects Creative Rhythms
- No subscription ticking during writing blocks
- Pay when you're ready to publish, not before
- Iterate freely without cost anxiety

### Clear Value Exchange
- Free = write and publish (with attribution)
- Paid = professional polish when you need it
- Not paying for permission to use your own work

### Export Flow

1. User clicks "Export PDF/EPUB"
2. Check if session is unlocked:
   - User has Pro subscription (active) → unlocked
   - User purchased this specific book → unlocked
   - Otherwise → show upgrade modal
3. If not unlocked: show modal with options
   - "Unlock this book — $12.99" (one-time)
   - "Go Pro — $39.99/year" (unlock everything)
4. If unlocked: generate clean export (no footer)

### Free Export Flow

1. User clicks "Export PDF/EPUB" (not unlocked)
2. Generate export with "Made with Hotpress" footer
3. Show post-export upsell: "Want clean exports? Unlock for $12.99"
